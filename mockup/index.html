    Scientific Warnings & Notes

      How these frames were made
      1. Asked the NASA/IPAC archive (IRSA) which SPHEREx images cover this position (DR1 + QR3).
      2. SPHEREx photographs each patch of sky dozens of times during every visit, each image in a slightly different colour. We keep up to 12 images per release, spread across the six bands and across the dates.
      3. Removed the zodiacal light model and put every image on one grid with north up. Between visits the spacecraft sees the sky rotated 180°; this undoes it.
      4. Applied the same brightness stretch to every image of a band, so blinking within a band stays fair.
      5. Moved Gaia DR3's position to each date (proper motion + parallax) and compared it with the measured centre of the star.
      6. The viewer opens on Band 6 (4.42–5.00 µm), or on the nearest band that has at least two images to blink. Images in different bands see different colours, so to judge a change in brightness, compare frames of one band.
